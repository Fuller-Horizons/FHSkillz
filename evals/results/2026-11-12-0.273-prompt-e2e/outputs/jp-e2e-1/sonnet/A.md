# Top 5 cost drivers

Ranked across COGS and operating expenses. Total costs are $856,200 against $800,700 of income, which gives the -$55,500 operating loss.

| Rank | Line item | Amount | % of revenue | % of total costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | COGS - Equipment | $231,900 | 29.0% | 27.1% |
| 2 | Payroll - Technicians | $198,500 | 24.8% | 23.2% |
| 3 | Payroll - Office | $71,200 | 8.9% | 8.3% |
| 4 | COGS - Subcontractors | $64,800 | 8.1% | 7.6% |
| 5 | Payroll Taxes & Benefits | $49,800 | 6.2% | 5.8% |
| | **Top 5 total** | **$616,200** | **77.0%** | **72.0%** |

I checked that the line items sum to the stated totals ($296,700 COGS and $559,500 expenses).

## Points for the owner conversation

- **Five lines drive 72% of spend.** Equipment and technician labor alone are 50% of all costs.
- **Labor is bigger than the ranking shows.** Technicians, office staff and taxes and benefits combine to $319,500, or 39.9% of revenue. Subcontractors would add $64,800 on top of that.
- **Equipment is the biggest lever.** At 29% of revenue, a 3-point improvement (through supplier pricing, markup discipline or job-costing) is worth about $24,000. That would close nearly half of the $55,500 loss.
- **Near-misses:**
  - Rent is #6 at $42,000.
  - Vehicle costs total $86,700 (fuel, lease and repairs). Grouped, they would rank #3 and are worth raising.
- **Sales mix:** Service plans are only 23.5% of income ($188,300 of $800,700). More recurring revenue could help cover the fixed labor base.
