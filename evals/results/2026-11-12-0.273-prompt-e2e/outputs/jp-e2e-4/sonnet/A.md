**Pick Quote A (VoiceLine Pro), as long as the service can move with you.**

**Monthly cost for 11 users, with recording**
- **A:** 11 × $27 = **$297/mo**. Recording is included.
- **B:** 11 × ($34 + $4 recording + $6 phone rental) = **$484/mo**.

B costs $187 more every month. B's lower upfront cost is a $2,079 saving on phones, less its $450 setup fee, so A catches up quickly.

**Total cost**

| Horizon | A (phones $2,079 + $297/mo) | B ($450 + $484/mo) |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months | $5,643 | $6,258 |
| 18 months (your move) | **$7,425** | $9,162 |
| 36 months | **$12,771** | $17,874 |

The break-even is about 9 months. After that, A is cheaper every month.

**The move is the real risk**
- **If the phones are cloud-based, A still wins.** You'd take the phones and the service to the new office, and A would be about $1,700 cheaper at month 18. VoIP generally works anywhere with internet.
- **If you'd have to cancel at month 18, A loses.** You'd owe the remaining 18 months, about $5,346. Your total would be about $12,771 against B's $9,162, so B would win by roughly $3,600.

**Before signing A, confirm in writing:**
1. The service and number transfer to a new address with no penalty.
2. You can reduce the user count if headcount changes.
3. Two auto-attendants cover your needs. B's unlimited auto-attendants only matter if you need a more complex call menu.
4. Whether the 36-month term renews automatically.

If A won't put the relocation terms in writing, ask for a 12-month term. If they refuse that too, B's flexibility is worth the premium. The cheaper choice is A only if you're confident the business will stay around and keep using the service for the full term.

I assumed the $189 phones are owned outright and that the termination fee covers service months only. I also assumed no taxes or fees beyond what's quoted.
