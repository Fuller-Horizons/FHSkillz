The file is `discovery-intake-questionnaire.doc`. It's an HTML-based `.doc`, which Word opens normally. I couldn't run python-docx here, so I didn't make a native `.docx`. If you want one, open it in Word and save it as `.docx`.

It takes the client about 40–50 minutes and has six sections:

1. **About the business:** work mix (residential vs. commercial, service vs. install vs. maintenance agreements), headcount by role, service area, revenue band, and revenue trend.
2. **Goals:** why they're reaching out now, what a good year looks like, a top-3 priority ranking, a 3–5 year direction, their biggest obstacle, and what they've already tried.
3. **Where money leaks:** a 20-item health check with a "don't know" option. It covers pricing and estimating, field productivity, sales and maintenance-agreement renewals, parts and warranty, billing and collections, and overhead. It also has a table of rough numbers (average ticket, margin, A/R days, callbacks, turnover, customer concentration) and a question on seasonality.
4. **How the business runs:** software in use, confidence in their numbers, who handles each function, and what breaks if the owner is out for four weeks.
5. **Readiness for change:** eight agree/disagree statements, how the last change went, who else needs to be involved, what worries them, and how urgent this is.
6. **Wrap-up:** a free-text catch-all, what would make the first meeting a success, and an optional document list (P&Ls, A/R aging, price book).

- **Open-ended questions:** these are deliberately few, and most items are checkboxes or ranges, so a busy owner can finish it.
- **"Don't know":** that answer is treated as useful data, since it often points to where money is leaking.
- **Return-by line:** the form has "2 business days before our meeting" built in.
- **Sensitive data:** the intro tells the client not to include SSNs, bank details or customer personal information.
- **Blanks to fill:** fill in the client name, return date, meeting date and return contact before sending.
