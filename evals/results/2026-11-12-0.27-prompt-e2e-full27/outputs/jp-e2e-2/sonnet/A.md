# Top complaints, ranked

## 1. Arrival times and no communication about delays (3 of 4 interviewees)
- **Dana:** "They said 9 to 11 and showed up at 1:40." She has to text the office to find out where the team is.
- **Marcus:** Arrival times also vary.
- **Tom:** "Nobody tells me when they're running late."

This is the most common complaint, and it hits weekly clients (Dana, Tom), who are likely your highest-value accounts. Two problems are mixed together here. The first is missing the window. The second is saying nothing when it's about to be missed. The second is cheaper to fix.

## 2. Different team every visit (2 of 4)
- **Priya:** "I have to explain the alarm code and the dog every single time."
- **Marcus:** He would pay more for the same team every time. This is a possible upsell, not just a complaint.

This one costs the client effort, and it also risks their safety and trust (alarm code, pet).

## 3. Missed spots on a deep clean (1 of 4)
- **Marcus:** "I'm paying for a deep clean and the shower glass still had spots." It happened twice.

This is lower frequency, but a repeat miss on a premium service is a churn risk. It may also be a side effect of #2, since a team that knows the home misses less.

## 4. Billing error (1 of 4)
- **Tom:** Double-charged once in March, and it took two calls to get the refund. Dana said billing is fine.

This is a one-off. The fix to look at is the refund process, because two calls to fix your own mistake is the real irritation.

## What's working
- **Quality:** Dana ("cleaners are great") and Tom are happy with it.
- **Online booking:** Priya likes it.

## What I'd fix first
1. **Send "on my way" and "running late" texts with an ETA.** This is cheap, addresses #1 directly, and would have solved Tom's complaint outright.
2. **Tighten the arrival windows or reset expectations.** Dana's 1:40 arrival against a 9–11 window is a dispatch problem, not a messaging one.
3. **Pilot a "preferred team" option, possibly as a paid tier.** It addresses #2, probably reduces #3, and Marcus has said he'd pay for it.
4. **Set up a refund process that closes in one contact.** This is low effort and fixes #4.

**Caveat:** This is only four interviews, so the counts are directional. Before committing resources, check the lateness and team-rotation patterns against your scheduling data. Both may come from the same root cause, which is how jobs are assigned and routed.
