# JAIL-BMC: Residential Pool Equipment Subscription, Long Island

**Contract:** Idea stage, no customers and no revenue. The canvas serves a go / no-go on spending money to test the idea. Research ran on 2026-11-12 and is mostly vendor blogs, so it is thin. Nothing below is VALIDATED, because there are no transactions, commitments or measured data. Every element is a HYPOTHESIS, with evidence strength and a confidence rating.

**Scope assumption to confirm:** I read "service pool equipment monthly" as a monthly technician visit to inspect and maintain pumps, filters, heaters and salt cells. I did not read it as weekly cleaning and chemicals, which is what most competitor pricing covers. That distinction drives several findings below.

## 2. Internal inconsistencies

1. **The value proposition and the price point may describe different products.** Most competitor pricing is for weekly cleaning and chemicals. A customer comparing your $89 to those numbers may expect cleaning. If the service doesn't include it, the offer looks like an incomplete version of what they know. If it does include it, one monthly visit cannot deliver it. You must pick one.
2. **A year-round subscription is attached to a seasonal asset.** Pools are open for about 5–7 months in the Northeast. A 12-month plan at $89 means $1,068 a year, which a customer sees as paying through winter for nothing. A season-only plan is about $534–623, which halves lifetime revenue. The customer's view of this is the likely churn driver.
3. **"Long Island" includes a market you may not be allowed to serve.** Suffolk has a clear licensing path. Nassau's rule, as reported, limits builders to servicing their own pools. Treat Nassau as out of scope until confirmed.
4. **Segments S1 and S2 need different propositions.** S1 buys prevention. S2 buys oversight. The canvas currently carries both under one price and one pitch.
5. **Referral partners are competitors.** Opening and closing companies and cleaning services already do equipment checks and upsell repairs. Your best channel could also be the most likely to retaliate.

## 3. Unit-economic dependencies

These are estimates (E) built on unverified inputs. Replace each with measured data before relying on it.

- **Revenue per customer:** $89/mo. Annual revenue is about $1,068 on a 12-month plan, or about $534–623 on a season-only plan.
- **Cost to serve (E):** If loaded tech labor is $1,200–1,800/wk, one tech day costs about $240–360. At 8 equipment visits a day, which is an assumption for Long Island traffic, labor is about $30–45 per visit. Add vehicle, insurance, parts and payment fees (E, about $10–15) and cost per visit is about $40–60. **Gross contribution is roughly $30–50 per customer-month, about 35–55% before overhead and CAC.**
- **Capacity (E):** 8 visits a day for about 20 working days is about 160 visits a month per tech. At 160 customers, revenue is about $14,000/mo against a loaded tech cost of about $5,200–7,800/mo. The model only works if the route is dense. At low density, drive time breaks it.
- **CAC ceiling (E):** At about $40 contribution a month, a 4-month payback allows CAC of about $160. No CAC data exists. Pool-adjacent paid-media costs on Long Island are unknown.
- **Scale dependency:** Break-even needs enough customers in a tight geography to fill at least one tech's days, plus fixed licensing, certification and insurance. The model does not work at 10–20 customers.

## 4. Red-team: what attacks this model

| Attack | Why it bites | Cheapest falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| **The segment doesn't feel the pain.** Owners mostly repair on failure and don't value prevention | Preventive equipment plans are an unproven consumer purchase here | 15 owner interviews: how many pay for equipment service today? |
| **The channel doesn't reach them cheaply.** Pool owners are a small share of households, and targeting is imprecise | CAC may exceed the ceiling | $500 geo-targeted test, measure cost per qualified lead |
| **Licensing blocks launch or adds a wait.** Cert plus 2 years' experience may rule you out until you hire | You may not be able to employ yourself as the tech | One call to Suffolk licensing |
| **Partners become competitors.** A referrer offers its own $89 plan and has the customer base | Incumbents have the relationship and the route | Ask 3 referrers whether they'd refer, and whether they already sell a plan |
| **Second-order response.** Incumbent cleaning companies add "equipment check" to their weekly visit at no extra charge | Your differentiator disappears at zero marginal cost to them | Ask existing cleaners whether they already include one |
| **Technician supply.** Certified techs are scarce and costly | Labor is your biggest cost and your growth limit | Price a hire; check market rate for a certified tech on LI |

## 5. Top assumptions and experiments

Ranked by impact if wrong times evidence weakness, and sequenced cheapest-decisive-first.

| # | Assumption | Experiment (jail-lab spec shape) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You can legally operate in Suffolk (and are or can hire a qualified tech)** | **Metric:** feasibility, yes/no, with cost and lead time. **Variable:** none (document check). **Bound:** one call plus a read of the county pages, about 2 hours, $0. **Pass:** a written license path with total cost under $5,000 and lead time under 90 days. **Fail:** any requirement you can't meet in-season. |
| 2 | **Cost to serve is under $50 per visit** | **Metric:** minutes door-to-door and total cost per visit (lower is better). **Variable:** the visit scope (a fixed 10-point equipment checklist). **Bound:** 10 visits with a paid subcontractor or friendly owners, 2 weeks, about $500. **Pass:** average ≤ 60 minutes including drive and loaded cost ≤ $50. |
| 3 | **Owners will pay $89/mo for the offer** | **Metric:** share of qualified owners who put down a refundable deposit or sign a commitment (higher is better). **Variable:** price at $89 vs. $69 vs. $109, one offer at a time. **Bound:** 40 qualified pool owners per price point, 3 weeks, about $300. **Pass:** ≥ 10% commit at $89. |
| 4 | **12-month billing is acceptable** | **Metric:** acceptance of a 12-month vs. season-only plan (higher is better). **Variable:** billing structure only, at a fixed $89. **Bound:** run as a split inside test 3. **Pass:** the 12-month plan converts within 70% of the season-only plan. |
| 5 | **CAC is at or below $160** | **Metric:** cost per committed customer from paid acquisition (lower is better). **Variable:** one channel (geo-targeted social). **Bound:** $500, 3 weeks, Suffolk only. **Pass:** CAC ≤ $160 on at least 3 conversions. **Caveat:** 3 conversions is a very small sample and is directional only. |

A KEEP on any of these promotes the matching element toward VALIDATED on the next canvas pass.

## 6. Customer validation questions

1. What did you spend on your pool equipment (repairs, replacements) in the last 2 years?
2. Who services your pool now, and what does that include? Does anyone check the equipment?
3. When did you last have an unplanned equipment failure, and what did it cost?
4. Would you pay $89 a month for a monthly visit? What would you expect it to include?
5. Would you pay year-round, or only April–October?
6. What would make you switch from your current provider?

## 7. Strategic implications and next actions

- **Do not build the full service yet.** The model is plausible but fully unproven, and its first gate (licensing) is binary and nearly free to check.
- **Run experiments 1 and 2 first.** Together they cost under $600 and decide whether the economics and the legality can work at all.
- **Resolve the cleaning-vs-equipment question before any pricing test.** Everything downstream depends on it.
- **Narrow to Suffolk and one town cluster.** Route density matters more than reach.
- **Treat the first 10–20 customers as a learning cohort, not a business.** The model cannot be profitable at that size.

**Biggest limits of this canvas:** pool counts, CAC and churn data were not found. The cost figures come from vendor blogs and a few of those sources look like SEO pages. Treat every number here as a placeholder until you've measured it.
