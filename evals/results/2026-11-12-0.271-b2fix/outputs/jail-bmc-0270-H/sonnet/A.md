**Ramp cost (the part that bites).** Assumes all new customers are incremental and ignores churn, van purchase and hiring costs.
- At 20 new regulars a month, van 3 breaks even in month 7. You lose about **$28,000** along the way.

## 3. Contradictions between blocks

1. **Revenue ↔ cost.** You are only about 33 customers above break-even on two vans. Any cost creep (fuel, insurance, a groomer raise) erases the margin. Expanding with no cushion is risky.
2. **Key resources ↔ the proposition.** The relationship runs through the groomer, but nothing shows you can hire a third groomer or keep customers when a groomer leaves.
3. **Costs ↔ capacity.** I can't tell whether $9,500 is fixed or partly per groom (commission). That changes whether a new customer costs $0 or real money at the margin.
4. **Segments ↔ channels.** You have 310 customers but no stated acquisition channel. I can't show the third van's customers will come from anywhere.
5. **Capacity.** If your vans have open slots, you don't need a third van yet. If they are full, the waitlist is the evidence you need. You haven't told me which.
