Each customer brings in about $48/month after cost to serve, you recover the $240 acquisition cost in 5 months, and a customer is worth roughly 6.7× what it costs to acquire. Treat that as a strong first read, not a finished one. The cost line is probably incomplete, and every figure is a blended average across three very different customer types. Three cheap data pulls will tell you whether the model holds.

## 2. Inconsistencies between blocks

1. **Three segments, one price, one cost.** Homeowners, HOAs, and motels buy different things (convenience, liability protection, guest-readiness). Yet the model carries a single $89 price and a single $41 cost. Shared and commercial pools are usually bigger and more demanding to service. If that holds here, the commercial 32% is either underpriced or the homeowner segment is subsidizing it. I can't tell which without segment data.
2. **The payer is not the user for HOAs.** The board or property manager decides, and a yard sign or a neighbor referral doesn't reach them. Your channel data doesn't say how the 68 commercial accounts were won.
3. **A cost line that likely omits your own labor.** You do 15 hours a week of route work. The $41 may not count it. Every $10/hour you value your time at adds about $3/customer/month, so $30/hour adds about $9.
4. **"Monthly subscription" versus Long Island seasonality.** Your data doesn't say whether customers pay year-round or whether winter cancellations hide inside the 3%. This is the first question your accountant will ask.
5. **Commercial compliance is unexamined.** I believe shared and commercial pools in New York carry health-department rules on water testing and logs. That is from memory, not verified, so treat it as H and check it before pitching the HOA and motel segments to an investor.

**Sensitivities (illustrative, not findings):**

| Scenario | LTV | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| As reported | $1,600 | 6.7× | 5.0 mo |
| Cost to serve really $55 | $1,133 | 4.7× | 7.1 mo |

The model stays viable under moderate error, but the combined case drops below the usual 3× comfort line. That is why the cost audit and the churn pull come first.

**Scale condition:** the 5-month payback assumes the $240 blended CAC holds as you grow. Referrals and yard signs, about half your volume, tend to saturate in a fixed geography. If they do, CAC rises toward the Google and partner level.

## 4. Red-team (inline)

| Attack | Why it could bite | Cheapest falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| The commercial segments don't pay enough | Bigger pools at the same $89. Boards and property managers rebid, and motels cut costs first in a bad season. | Time 5 stops per segment for 2 weeks and compare. |
| The channel doesn't reach the commercial payer | Yard signs and referrals are residential. | Ask each HOA and motel customer how they found you. |
| The partnership is a dependency | About 17% of customers come from one store, and its terms are unknown. The store could start its own service or favor a rival. | Ask what the arrangement is and whether it's written down. |
| Second-order competitor response | Larger local pool companies can price-match or bundle opening and closing. | Look at what 3 local competitors publish for monthly rates and what's included. |
| Key-person risk | You are 15 hrs/week of route capacity plus the owner. A busy season or illness hits service quality directly. | List what breaks if you take 2 weeks off. |

## 5. Top assumptions by risk, with experiments (cheapest and most decisive first)

Thresholds below are my proposals. Adjust them with your accountant before you run anything.

| # | Assumption | Experiment (jail-lab spec) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost to serve is $41 or less, fully loaded, and holds in every segment | **Metric:** fully loaded contribution per customer-month by segment (↑). **Variable:** segment (costing basis fixed: tech time, drive time, your hours at an agreed rate, chemicals, parts). **Bound:** 2 to 4 weeks of timesheets and truck logs, about $0. **Pass:** every segment ≥ $30. **Fail:** any segment < $20, which means reprice or exit it. |
| 2 | Churn is about 3% and stable by segment, channel, and through winter | **Metric:** monthly churn (↓). **Variable:** cohort cut. **Bound:** one billing-data pull, about an hour. **Pass:** no segment above 4%/month, no channel cohort above 5%, winter months under 2× average. |
| 3 | CAC is $240 and stays near that by channel | **Metric:** CAC per channel, with spend, your time, and partner payouts (↓). **Variable:** channel. **Bound:** one day, from receipts and notes. **Pass:** every channel LTV:CAC ≥ 3×. |
| 4 | $89 isn't leaving money on the table | **Metric:** quote-to-signup conversion (hold or ↑). **Variable:** price quoted to new homeowner leads ($99 versus $89, alternated by week). **Bound:** 60 days, about 50 quotes, existing customers untouched. **Pass:** conversion at $99 ≥ 85% of conversion at $89. Break-even is about 83%. At this sample size the result is directional only. |
| 5 | The partner channel can be repeated and isn't a single point of failure | **Metric:** signed customers and CAC from a second pool-supply store (↑ customers, ↓ CAC). **Variable:** adding one more store on the same terms. **Bound:** 60 days, one store. **Pass:** ≥ 3 signed customers at CAC ≤ $240. |

Run 1 to 3 in the first two weeks, since they are data pulls. Run 4 and 5 in parallel over the following 60 days. Once you run these through jail-lab, a KEEP result moves the matching hypothesis toward VALIDATED on the next canvas pass.

## 6. Customer validation questions

- **Homeowners, including 5 who left:** What problem were you solving by hiring us? What would make you cancel? Why did the ones who left leave?
- **HOA boards or property managers:** Who decides on pool service, and how often is it rebid? What documentation do you need from the vendor? What would you pay for it?
- **Motels and B&Bs:** What does a closed or unready pool cost you in a busy week? What do you do in the off-season?
- **Pool-supply store:** Why do you refer us? What's in it for you? Could you or someone else do the same?

## 7. What this means and what to do next

1. **Don't increase acquisition spend yet.** Run experiments 1 to 3 first. They cost about nothing and decide whether 6.7× is real.
2. **If any segment falls below the thresholds,** reprice or stop selling to it, and consider splitting the pricing by segment.
3. **Test price.** On your numbers, a $10 increase adds about 21% to per-customer contribution, which makes it the highest-leverage lever on the canvas.
4. **Start a second partner pilot** to reduce channel dependency.
5. **For your accountant,** bring:
   - The billing export, with monthly active counts and cancellations.
   - A cost breakdown behind the $41, showing whether your 15 hours are in it.
   - How CAC was calculated.
   - Whether revenue is year-round.
   - Overhead and owner pay.
6. **For an investor,** my read is that this is a profitable, cash-generating local route business. It becomes an investable story only if segment data shows a scalable wedge, such as commercial accounts with higher margins or route density that lowers cost per stop. I'd hold the investor conversation until experiments 1 to 3 are in.
