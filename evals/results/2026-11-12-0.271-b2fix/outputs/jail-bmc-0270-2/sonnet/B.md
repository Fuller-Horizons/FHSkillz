- **The model looks healthy on the blended numbers.** The usual target is 3 to 1 or better.

- **Growth has a ceiling.** At about 14 new customers a month and 3% churn, you level off near **466 customers** (14 ÷ 0.03). At 212 you lose about 6 a month, so you need about 6 new customers a month just to stay flat. Getting to 300 takes about 14 months at this pace. This is an Estimate.

## Where the blocks disagree

1. **One price and one cost for three different segments.** Pool service for a motel or an HOA is a different job from a backyard pool, yet you gave me a single $89 and a single $41. Either all three pay $89, which is likely too low for commercial work, or $89 is an average I can't break apart.
2. **The channels fit homeowners.** Yard signs and neighbor referrals reach neighbors. I don't know how the 31% of customers who are HOAs and motels found you.
3. **The $240 CAC may hide the real cost.** Referrals are cheap and scale only with density. If growth needs more paid channels, the next customer costs more than the average.
4. **The owner's 15 hours a week may be missing from costs.** If that time is not inside the $41, margin is overstated. Every $1,000 a month of unpaid owner labor lowers margin by about $4.70 per customer.
5. **Lifetime is extrapolated.** The data covers 18 months and one winter. I don't know whether churn differs by season or by segment.

## Attacking the model

- **A segment that won't pay.** HOA boards buy by bid and change members. Motels cut costs in the off-season. Both may churn faster than homeowners.
- **A channel that doesn't reach them.** Only the Google channel plausibly reaches commercial buyers.
- **A partnership that is really a dependency.** The store supplies about 2 to 3 new customers a month with no terms on file. It could start its own service or favor a competitor.
- **Second-order competitor response.** A bigger firm or the store itself could undercut $89 in your best neighborhoods.
- **Other risks.** Commercial pools may carry health-code and insurance requirements that I did not research. Check them for Suffolk and Nassau. Key-person risk is also real, because you are doing route work.

## Riskiest assumptions and the experiments to test them
These run cheapest and most decisive first. Thresholds are my proposals, and you can adjust them.

| # | Assumption | Experiment | Pass threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Churn stays near 3% in every segment and season | **Metric:** monthly churn by segment and signup month (lower is better). **Variable:** segment. **Run:** your full 18-month billing export, about 2 hours, $0. | Every segment at or below 3.5%. Any segment above 5% means the blend is hiding a problem. |
| 2 | The $240 CAC is complete | **Metric:** fully loaded CAC by channel (lower is better). **Variable:** channel. **Run:** re-cut the last 12 months, including signs, store perks, ad spend and your selling time, about 1 weekend. | Blended CAC at or below $380 (payback within 8 months). |
| 3 | The $41 cost to serve is complete in every segment | **Metric:** fully loaded margin per customer (higher is better). **Variable:** segment. **Run:** log time, miles and parts for 4 weeks, valuing owner time at a market technician rate. | At least $27 a month in each segment (payback within 9 months). |
| 4 | HOAs and motels accept a higher price | **Metric:** quote acceptance rate (higher is better). **Variable:** price only. **Run:** the next 10 commercial quotes, 5 at $89 and 5 at $129, over 60 days, $0. | Acceptance at $129 is at least 60% of acceptance at $89. The sample is small, so treat a pass as a signal, not proof. |
| 5 | Acquisition does not depend on the store and Google | **Metric:** new subscribers per $1,000 from one new channel (higher is better). **Variable:** the channel. **Run:** 30 days, budget set by you. | At least 2 new subscribers at CAC of $380 or less. Separately, get the store's terms in writing. |

## Questions to ask customers
- **Homeowners:** What would make you cancel? What did you do before us, and what did it cost?
- **HOA boards:** Who decides, how are vendors chosen, and what is the contract term?
- **Motels and B&Bs:** Do you pause service in winter? What pool rules do you follow?
- **Referred customers:** Why did you sign up?
- **Churned customers:** Why did you leave?

## What to do next
1. **Do experiments 1 and 2 first.** They cost only time. They tell you and your investor whether 6.7 to 1 holds up.
2. **Split the numbers by segment.** One blended view hides the most important risk, which is whether HOAs and motels earn their keep.
3. **Show your accountant these three figures:** loaded cost to serve, loaded CAC, and overhead plus owner pay. With those you can state real profit.
4. **Fix the growth ceiling.** Lowering churn from 3% to 2% raises lifetime to 50 months and the ceiling to about 700 customers. That is likely cheaper than finding more new customers.
5. **Get the store partnership in writing.**

**Stress test, not a forecast.** If margin falls to $38, CAC rises to $360 and churn rises to 4%, then LTV is about $950, the ratio is 2.6 to 1 and payback is 9.5 months. That is still workable.
