**Why A wins on cost:** B costs $187 more per month. That is about $2,244 a year, or $5,100 over 36 months. B's recording add-on and phone rental erase the difference in setup and hardware cost within the first year.

**Where B can win:** A's contract is $12,771 whether you stay or leave. B costs $450 plus $484 per month. The break-even is about 25 months. If there's a real chance you'd cancel before then, B is cheaper. Examples are leaving the provider at the move or shrinking the team. For example, if you cancelled at month 18, A would cost $12,771 against B's $9,162, a $3,609 penalty.

**The move itself isn't a problem if the service is cloud-based.** Most hosted phone systems (VoIP) move with you. You plug the phones in at the new office and keep your numbers. In that case the 18-month move doesn't trigger A's termination fee.

**Before signing A:**
1. Ask VoiceLine in writing whether service and numbers transfer to a new address, and whether there are any relocation fees.
2. Ask for a 12 or 24-month term. At 24 months, the worst-case exposure drops substantially.
3. Check that 2 auto-attendants is enough. B's unlimited auto-attendants only matter if you need more than 2, for example separate menus for sales, support and after-hours.
4. Ask whether the phones are standard SIP models you could reuse elsewhere. You own them, so they keep some value.

**Choose B instead if** VoiceLine won't confirm portability, or if you might change providers or restructure at the move. Then B's flexibility is worth the extra $187 per month.
