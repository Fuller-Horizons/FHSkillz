## Top complaints from the four interviews, ranked

**1. Arrival times are unreliable and nobody warns clients (3 of 4 interviews)**
- Dana: *"They said 9 to 11 and showed up at 1:40." Has to text the office to find out.*
- Marcus: *"Arrival times also vary."*
- Tom: *"Nobody tells me when they're running late."*

This is the most common complaint and the most consistent. The fix is probably communication more than scheduling: proactive "on the way" and "running late" notices would address Tom's complaint directly and reduce the office calls Dana describes.

**2. Crews change every visit, so clients repeat their instructions (2 of 4)**
- Priya: *"I have to explain the alarm code and the dog every single time."*
- Marcus: *"Would pay more for same team every time."*

Marcus's comment is a revenue signal, not just a complaint. A stable team assignment or a shared client-notes file (alarm codes, pets, access, preferences) that crews see before arriving could fix both.

**3. Missed spots on deep cleans (1 of 4, but on a premium service)**
- Marcus: *"I'm paying for a deep clean and the shower glass still had spots."*

Only one client raised this, but it hit a paid premium service and it recurred ("twice"). A checklist or a final walkthrough for deep cleans is a cheap fix to try.

**4. Billing error took two calls to resolve (1 of 4)**
- Tom was double-charged in March and was refunded only *"after two calls."*

This is a single incident, and Dana says billing is fine. The refund worked, so the issue is the recovery process. Before treating it as a priority, check whether duplicate charges happen more broadly.

## Recommendation

Fix in this order:
1. **Late and arrival-window communication.** It is the most frequent complaint, it has a clear root cause, and it is likely the cheapest to fix.
2. **Crew continuity and client notes.** It addresses two clients' complaints and Marcus's willingness to pay more.
3. **Deep-clean quality check.** A low-cost pilot that addresses the premium-service risk.
4. **Duplicate-charge check.** Look at the billing data before deciding whether this needs more than the refund process.

These are four interviews, so treat the counts as directional. A wider sample would show whether the arrival-window pattern holds.
