# Scope Draft: Landscaping Marketing Engagement

**Status:** Draft for discussion. Nothing is final until we agree on it.

## 1. The problem, in the owner's terms

"Fixing marketing" needs a definition before we commit to a plan. Discovery (Phase 1) will confirm which of these is the real problem:

- Not enough calls or form fills
- Calls come in but estimates don't get booked
- Estimates go out but don't close
- Not enough repeat or referral work
- Too much spend for the work it brings in

Each one leads to different fixes, so we'll pick one primary problem before executing.

## 2. Goal and success measures

**Goal:** Increase qualified spring leads and booked estimates before the spring season, at a sustainable cost per job.

| Measure | Baseline | Target by April 30 |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified leads per month (calls + form fills) | Measured in Phase 1 | Target set after Phase 1, agreed in writing |
| Estimates booked per month | Measured in Phase 1 | Target set after Phase 1 |
| Google Business Profile reviews (count and average rating) | Measured in Phase 1 | Target set after Phase 1 |
| Cost per booked estimate | Measured in Phase 1 | Target set after Phase 1 |

We will not promise a revenue number. Lead and booking volume depends on demand, crew capacity, pricing, and how the sales team follows up, and some of that sits outside marketing.

## 3. Timeline

Landscaping demand builds from January to March, so the work has to be ready by mid-January.

- **Phase 1: Discovery and baseline (Weeks 1–2, late Nov to early Dec).** Audit, interviews, and measurement.
- **Phase 2: Foundation (Weeks 3–6, December).** Fix the things that cost the most to leave broken.
- **Phase 3: Launch (January to mid-February).** Campaigns and follow-up go live.
- **Phase 4: Spring push and optimization (mid-February to April).** Adjust based on data.

## 4. Deliverables

**Phase 1: Discovery ($900)**
- Audit of Google Business Profile, website, review profile, and listings on directories
- Review of the last 12 months of leads and jobs by service, source, and month
- 30-minute interview with the owner and whoever answers the phone and does estimates
- Written baseline report with the primary problem identified and recommended priorities

**Phase 2: Foundation ($1,800)**
- Google Business Profile rebuilt: categories, services, photos, and posting schedule
- Review request process: a simple script and a text or email sequence to recent customers
- Call and lead tracking set up so every lead has a source
- Website fixes limited to the top 3 issues found in the audit (for example, mobile, phone click-to-call, service pages). **A full website rebuild is not included.**
- Estimate follow-up sequence drafted (calls and emails for estimates not yet answered)

**Phase 3: Launch ($1,800)**
- Spring offer and messaging for 3 core services, agreed with the owner
- Local campaign setup (Google Local Services Ads if eligible, or Google Search, limited to the service area)
- Simple monthly dashboard: leads, sources, bookings, reviews, cost per booking

**Phase 4: Spring push and optimization ($1,000)**
- Weekly 30-minute check-in during the active season
- Campaign and page changes based on data
- End-of-season report with what worked, what didn't, and recommendations for next year

**Contingency ($500)**
- Used only with the owner's written approval for work outside the plan above

**Total: $6,000**

## 5. Not included

- Ad spend (see section 6)
- Full website redesign or new development
- Logo, brand, or print design
- Staff training on sales calls (we can recommend a process, but not deliver training hours)
- Social media management beyond the Google Business Profile
- Guaranteed rankings, lead counts, or revenue
- Hiring or managing outside vendors, unless agreed in writing

## 6. Ad budget (decision needed)

Paid advertising is usually a separate line from the consulting fee. We need to agree on:

- **Who pays for ads, and how much a month.** We'll propose a test budget after Phase 1, based on the cost per booked estimate the data shows.
- **Who holds the ad accounts.** The owner should own them, with our access.
- **Stop rule.** If cost per booked estimate stays above an agreed ceiling for an agreed number of weeks, we pause and review together.

## 7. What we need from the owner

- Access to Google Business Profile, website admin, and any directory accounts (as owner or manager)
- Access to call logs, lead sources, and job or estimate records, even if they're rough
- One decision-maker to approve offers and changes within 2 business days
- Weekly 30-minute check-ins during the active season
- Fast follow-up on leads, since slow response is often the biggest lever

## 8. Risks

- **Spring demand arrives late or weak.** Weather and local economy affect timing, so we'll track leads weekly, not just the final result.
- **Slow or missed follow-up on calls and estimates.** This can cancel out better marketing. We'll flag it early.
- **Crew capacity limits.** Extra demand is useless if the schedule is full in April. We need the owner's view of capacity by service.

## 9. Open questions before we sign

1. What's the main complaint right now: too few leads, or leads that don't book?
2. What services make the most money, and which ones do you want more of?
3. What's your service area, and is it limited by drive time or by county?
4. What's your monthly ad budget today, if any, and what has it produced?
5. Who answers the phone and does estimates?
6. What are your spring and summer capacity limits by service?
7. Do you have a CRM, or is it a notebook and text messages?
8. What's the most you can spend per month on ads once the engagement ends?
