# DRAFT Task Contract: Landscaping Marketing Engagement

**Mode:** default lane. This is **not final**: fields 1, 3, 12 and 13 depend on four questions at the end. I've marked those UNKNOWN rather than guess.

**1. Objective:** Turn "fix marketing" into a booked-jobs problem: more qualified estimate requests landing in Jan–Mar, so the spring calendar fills at good margin.

**2. Stakeholder:** The owner, who is deciding where to put marketing effort and money before the spring rush.

**3. Required outcome:** UNKNOWN. I need a number: estimate requests, booked jobs, or revenue (Q1).

**4. Deliverables** (fixed set, nothing else):
- **Phase 1, Diagnostic (weeks 1–2):** a 1-page "what's actually broken" findings memo. It covers lead sources, conversion from inquiry to estimate to job, and the Google Business Profile, website and review state.
- **Phase 2, Fix and launch (weeks 3–8):**
  - Google Business Profile cleanup and a review-request process.
  - A one-page offer and landing page for 1–2 priority services, with a quote form and call tracking.
  - A lead follow-up script and speed-to-response rule.
  - A simple lead log that tracks source through to booked job.
  - A 90-day spring campaign plan with the ad budget recommendation, covering email or SMS to past customers and any local paid ads.
- **Phase 3, Handoff (weeks 9–10):** a 1-page dashboard and a "run it yourself" checklist.

**5. Constraints:**
- About $6k of consulting fees (my fee, not ad spend).
- Spring results mean work must be live by about mid-January. Today is Nov 12, so we have roughly 9 weeks.
- Client lead and customer data is confidential and handled by class only.

**6. Non-goals:**
- No ad spend inside the $6k.
- No rebrand, logo or full website rebuild.
- No ongoing social media posting or management.
- No guaranteed lead or revenue numbers.
- No SEO beyond the Google Business Profile and basic on-page fixes.

**7. Inputs available:** The owner's request, and the budget and timing.

**8. Inputs missing** (the owner provides):
- Google Business Profile, website and analytics access.
- Last 12 months of jobs and revenue by service.
- Current lead sources, and any marketing spend and what it produced.
- Who answers the phone and quotes, and their capacity limits.
- Past-customer list, in whatever form exists.

**9. Assumptions** (each is wrong if the diagnostic says otherwise):
- The problem is lead flow and conversion, not capacity or pricing. If the crew is already full, the goal changes to margin or job mix, which is a new contract.
- Residential maintenance and design/install are the main revenue. If commercial dominates, the offer and channels change.
- There is no usable tracking today. If there is, Phase 1 is shorter.
- The owner can approve decisions within 2 business days. Slower approvals push out the launch date.

**10. Dependencies:** Owner access and approvals, the owner or staff completing reviews and follow-up, and a web or form tool for the landing page. The 9-week window needs the campaign live by mid-January.

**11. Material risks:**
- **Seasonality:** if the work slips past January, the main booking window is missed. The consequence is paying for fixes that don't help until next year.
- **Execution after handoff:** a plan nobody runs produces nothing. The consequence is the client blaming the work. The mitigation is naming one internal owner for follow-up now.
- **Vague success:** without a Field 3 number, "results" gets judged by gut feel.
- **Ad dollars:** paid ads need their own budget, or they will be cut from the plan.

**12. Success criteria:** UNKNOWN until Field 3 is set. The proposed leading indicators are: 100% of leads logged with source, a median response time under 1 hour during business hours, review count up by an agreed target, and a baseline-versus-Q1 comparison of estimate requests.

**13. Completion criteria (testable, would FAIL if work is wrong):**
- Phase 1 memo delivered, and the owner confirms the findings.
- A test inquiry through the form and a test call both appear in the lead log with the correct source.
- The Google Business Profile shows the corrected info, and the review-request link works.
- The landing page is live before mid-January.
- The owner can show the dashboard and explain it unaided in the handoff session.

**14. Approval-required actions:**
- Any change to the live website or Google Business Profile.
- Any spend or ad account setup.
- Messages sent to customers (email or SMS).
- Any access to or sharing of customer data.
- Any change beyond Field 4.

**Change control:** additions that touch Field 4 are logged and re-priced. Changes to the goal in Fields 1–3 get a new contract.
