Argue against blue and you're left with "it's the default, so it blends in." Argue against green and you get "it can read as cheap or eco-themed, and light greens fail contrast." Neither is decisive, so the choice should depend on your brand and on the contrast test.

- **Use the brand color if Signal Results Group has one.** That beats either option on its own.
- **Keep body text near-black or dark navy.** Color should be an accent, not the text.
- **Put the accent on the name, a divider, or links, and only if it passes contrast.** Use a dark green or dark blue, around 4.5:1 against white. Bright greens often fail, and that's the biggest practical risk with green.
- **Watch links.** Most email clients render links in blue, so a blue signature can make links look like the rest of the text. Green avoids that clash.
- **Check dark mode.** Many clients invert colors, and a dark green can turn muddy.

For a consulting firm, my pick is dark navy text with a deep green accent for the name or divider. It reads as established and still looks distinct from the blue most consultants use. If you have no brand palette, I'd go with deep green (not bright) for the accent, unless you want the safest possible choice, which is dark blue.
