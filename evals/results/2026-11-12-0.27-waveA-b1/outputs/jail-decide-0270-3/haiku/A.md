**Options**

| | A. Buy Router X | B. Lease Router Y | C. Keep current router |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | $64,000 | Lease terms to confirm | $0 |
| Monthly cost | None | $1,350 | Repairs as they occur (~$770/mo this year) |
| 5-year total | $64,000 | $81,000 | Repairs continue and likely rise with age |
| Service | Not included in the analysis | Service plan included | Ad hoc |
| Uptime | New machine after 10-week lead time | New machine after lead time | Declining; four repairs this year |

**Analysis**
- **Total cost:** The lease costs $17,000 more over five years (27%) than buying outright.
- **What the premium buys:** The lease is equivalent to borrowing $64,000 at roughly 9.7% annual interest, before counting the included service plan. Leasing makes sense if the $64,000 we keep in cash earns more than that, or if we would otherwise pay more than that to borrow.
- **Cash:** Buying would use cash we need for inventory. That is the main reason to lease.
- **Current machine:** Repairs cost about $770 a month on average this year, and the lease payment is $1,350. The lease therefore costs about $580 a month more than current repairs, before counting downtime. The repair trend is rising with the machine's age.

**Recommendation:** Lease Router Y, subject to the conditions below.

1. Confirm the service plan covers wear parts, labor, and response time.
2. Confirm lease terms: upfront payment, end-of-term options, early buyout cost, and fees.
3. Confirm the 10-week lead time and plan how the current router will be kept running until delivery.
