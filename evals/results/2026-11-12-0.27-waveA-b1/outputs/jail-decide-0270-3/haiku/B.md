# Decision Memo: Replace the Aging CNC Router

**To:** Partners
**From:** [Your name]
**Date:** Nov 10
**Decision requested:** Approve leasing Router Y at $1,350/month for 60 months, in place of buying Router X for $64,000 cash.

**Background**
Our 2011 CNC router has needed four repairs this year, costing about $9,200 in total. A new unit has a 10-week lead time.

**Options**
- **A. Buy Router X:** $64,000 cash.
- **B. Lease Router Y:** $1,350/month for 60 months ($81,000 in total payments), including a service plan.

**Comparison**

| Criterion | A: Buy Router X | B: Lease Router Y |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | $64,000 | Not stated in quote [confirm] |
| Monthly cost | None (ownership) | $1,350 |
| 5-year payments | $64,000 | $81,000 |
| Repairs and service | Not in quote [confirm] | Service plan included |
| Uptime commitment | Not quantified | Covered by service plan [confirm SLA] |
| Delivery | 10 weeks | [confirm] |

**Analysis**
- Buying is $17,000 cheaper in nominal payments over five years, about $283/month.
- Leasing keeps $64,000 in cash available for inventory.
- The lease's service plan may replace the roughly $9,200/year we are spending on repairs of the current router. Buying would leave us to fund maintenance on Router X ourselves.
- The break-even rate is about 9.7% a year. If the cash we would otherwise spend on the purchase is worth more than that to us (for inventory or other uses), leasing is the lower-cost option. If not, buying is cheaper.
- Not yet in the numbers: the resale value of Router X after five years, the cost of a service plan for Router X, and any early-termination penalty on the lease.

**Recommendation**
Lease Router Y, subject to confirming the items marked [confirm] above before signing.

**Approval requested**
Authorize us to execute the 60-month lease and schedule delivery. The lease is a binding multi-year commitment, so please confirm approval before we sign.
