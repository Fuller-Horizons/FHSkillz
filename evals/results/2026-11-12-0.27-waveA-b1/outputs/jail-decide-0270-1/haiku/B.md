**Buy the dispatch software, on a short trial, and decide the hire by early January based on what the software does to overtime.** Keeping things as they are is the worst option.

| Option | Year-one cost | Ongoing cost | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep going | $21,600 overtime | $21,600/yr | Nothing. Overtime and burnout continue. |
| Software | $7,800 ($4,800 + $3,000 setup) | $4,800/yr | Targets the overtime directly. |
| Second dispatcher | About $52,000 | About $52,000/yr | Adds capacity, but costs about $2,500/month more than the overtime it would replace. |

**Why software:**
- It breaks even in year one if it cuts overtime by about a third ($7,200 saved vs. $7,800 spent). If it cuts overtime in half, you save about $13,800 in year one and $16,800 a year after that.
- It's cheap to reverse. A hire is hard to undo, and a 12-person shop can't absorb a bad hire easily.
- If your overtime is seasonal, a $52k salary runs all year for a problem that may not. Software scales with use.

**Why not the hire yet:** $52k only pays off if the extra capacity brings in revenue. At a 35–40% gross margin, one dispatcher has to unlock roughly $130k–$150k a year in jobs you're currently turning away or losing. You may be there, but you need to check before committing.

**Before December 15, pull two weeks of data:**
1. **Missed or unanswered calls.** If calls go to voicemail or get dropped during the crunch, the bottleneck is intake, and software or an answering service helps. If calls are answered but jobs stay unbooked, the constraint is scheduling capacity.
2. **Jobs you turned away or pushed out.** Multiply by your average job margin. That's the real cost of the bottleneck, which is probably well above $21,600.
3. **How the dispatcher's time splits** between phones, scheduling, and tech communication. Software mainly helps the scheduling and tech-comms share.

**Negotiate the software deal:** ask for the setup fee to be waived or reduced, and insist on a 60–90 day trial or month-to-month terms rather than an annual lock-in. Also check that it integrates with your current job-management system.

**Change my recommendation if:**
- Your data shows lost revenue above about $130k a year, which makes a hire justified even at $52k.
- The software can't cut overtime by at least a third after 60 days of real use.
- Call volume is the real constraint. Then an answering service (typically a few hundred dollars a month) may beat both options.
