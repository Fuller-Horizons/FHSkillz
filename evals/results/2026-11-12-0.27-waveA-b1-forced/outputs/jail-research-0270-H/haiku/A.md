## Answer

**Federal minimum wage: $7.25/hour.** It has been unchanged since July 24, 2009, and no increase has been enacted since [4][6]. Fact, high confidence. The DOL page itself was not reachable, so this rests on search excerpts of it and on two independent secondary sources.

**New York on Long Island: $17.00/hour in 2026, so yes, it differs.** Nassau and Suffolk sit in NY's downstate tier, which also includes NYC and Westchester, at $17.00 effective January 1, 2026. The rest of the state is $16.00 [1]. That is $9.75/hour (about 134%) above the federal floor. Fact, medium-high confidence. For a Long Island worksite, the $17.00 rate is the one that applies, since the region is set by where the employee works [1].

**For the payroll budget:** use $17.00/hour as the 2026 floor for Long Island staff. The 2027 figure is not confirmed. New York's rate is set to index to the three-year moving average of the Northeast CPI-W from January 1, 2027, with an off-ramp for certain economic conditions [2]. The sources split: most say indexing begins in 2027 [1][2], and one says no 2027 increase is scheduled. I could not confirm which is right. Unknown until the NYSDOL minimum wage page is checked. As a planning scenario only (Estimate, my arithmetic, not an official forecast): if the indexed increase were 3%, the Long Island rate would be about $17.51/hour, roughly $0.51 higher. The real adjustment will depend on the three-year average and the off-ramp.
