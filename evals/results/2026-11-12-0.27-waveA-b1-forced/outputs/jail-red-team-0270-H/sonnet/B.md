# RED-TEAM: First salesperson, 300 dormant customers, 4-month payback

**Lane: THREE LENSES.** The commitment is about $80k a year plus recruiting, and it can be undone in a quarter at a real but survivable cost.

**Steelman.** The 300 dormant customers are a warm, owned list of people who already paid you once. That makes them cheaper to reach than cold leads. Focused daily outreach by a dedicated person should surface revenue that owner-led selling never gets to. Base plus commission keeps the downside bounded and ties pay to results. Four months is a short window that forces a go/no-go.

## RED-TEAM FINDINGS (ranked by severity × likelihood)

**1. The payback bar is high, and the clock isn't defined.** Severity: **critical**. Basis: arithmetic plus my own rough prior. I have no data on your business, so treat the assumptions as mine.
- Loaded base cost (about 20% burden) is roughly $26k over 4 months.
- Add recruiting, tools and ramp, and the hard cost is about $30–35k, before commission.
- At roughly 35% margin after commission, that needs about **$90k of collected revenue in about 12 productive weeks**. That is about $300 per account across all 300, or about $3k each if 10% reactivate.
- Recruiting takes 4–8 weeks and onboarding 2–4 more. A 4-month clock that starts at the offer date leaves about 2 months of real selling.
- Dormant lists often reach low double-digit to ~30% of contacts and reactivate in low single digits to ~10%. That is my prior, not a sourced benchmark.
- "Pays for herself" is also undefined: cash breakeven, gross profit covering salary, or monthly run-rate? With no definition, you can't fail it.
- Fix: write one definition and the start date before you hire.
- Falsifier: run the break-even with your real average order value and gross margin. Status: **RUN-NOW → result: inconclusive on feasibility, since it needs your AOV and margin, but the bar is confirmed at about $90k collected in about 12 weeks under the assumptions above.**

**2. One finite list is the entire pipeline.** Severity: **critical**. Basis: mechanism. At 30–50 dials a day, 300 accounts get a full first pass in 2–3 weeks. After that she is re-calling the same people or cold-calling with no lead source, no offer and no playbook.
- Fix: name the month 2+ lead source, such as referrals, inbound, or enrichment of the list.
- Falsifier: owner or ops counts how many of the 300 have a verified working contact, and how many are dead, closed or reachable only through a gatekeeper. Status: **HANDOFF → owner, a 30-minute CRM list audit.**

**3. You don't know why they went dormant.** Severity: **major**. Basis: selection bias. These are the customers who left or stopped buying. The reasons might be price, service failure, a competitor, closure, or simply that the need was one-time, and each one needs a different pitch. A salesperson can't fix a bad product or a one-time need.
- Fix: segment by last purchase date, lifetime value and exit reason.
- Falsifier: before posting the job, the owner personally calls the top 20–30 dormant accounts by lifetime value for 2 weeks and records reach rate, stated reason, and any interest or order. Status: **HANDOFF → owner. Lab spec: metric = reactivation conversations per 25 calls, pass if ≥3 expressions of interest.** This is also the cheapest test of the whole plan.

**4. Revenue lag and sales cycle.** Severity: **major**. Basis: mechanism. If an order needs a quote, a site visit, onboarding or net-60 payment, orders booked in month 3 don't arrive as cash by month 4. Hire-to-payback may really need 6–9 months.
- Fix: state your typical days from first contact to cash.
- Falsifier: pull the last 10 orders and measure days from first touch to payment. Status: **HANDOFF → owner or bookkeeper, 20 minutes.**

**5. A first-sales-hire setup that makes failure hard to diagnose.** Severity: **major**. Basis: reasoning. If the founder has never sold through a repeatable process, there is nothing for her to execute.
- Missing: script and offer, CRM, a manager, call targets, ramp period, a rule for who owns accounts, and a commission design. If she revives customers who would have come back anyway, she gets paid for a windfall.
- Fix: write the offer, a 90-day activity target and a commission rule (new or reactivated revenue only, with a defined lookback, such as no orders in the last 12 months).
- Falsifier: ask whether someone other than you could run this call tomorrow from a one-page guide. Status: **RUN-NOW → result: confirmed gap**, because no script, offer, targets or commission rule were stated in the plan.

**6. The options nobody wrote down.** Severity: **minor**. Basis: lens 1. Alternatives are cheaper to test first:
- Do nothing.
- The owner calls the list for 2 weeks.
- Email or SMS reactivation.
- A part-time or contract SDR.
- A commission-heavy structure with a lower base.

If any calls reach consumers, compliance (TCPA, do-not-call lists) also applies. Falsifier: any of these produces a reactivation rate by itself. Status: **HANDOFF → owner, same test as finding 3.**

## Scenario spread
- **Best:** a good list, a short cycle and a decent AOV mean about 10% reactivate, and she breaks even in about 5 months.
- **Expected:** low single-digit reactivation and a 60–90-day cycle give breakeven in about 8–12 months, so the 4-month test reads as a failure.
- **Worst:** the list is stale, reactivation is near zero, you absorb about $35–45k and the hire is a mismatch.

## Invalidation conditions
Set these at week 4, not month 4. Thresholds are placeholders until finding 1's numbers are real.
- Fewer than about 25% of the list reached.
- Fewer than about 10 qualified conversations.
- No pipeline of roughly $30k or more.

If any of these trips, stop or restructure.

**Survives the attack:** the idea of using the dormant list as a first revenue source is sound. It is cheap, warm and owned. A dedicated person is also a legitimate way to test it. What fails is the 4-month payback expectation and treating the list as a full pipeline.

**Verdict: PROCEED-WITH-FIXES.** The hire isn't the flaw. The undefined 4-month payback and the single finite list are. Before you sign an offer, run the 2-week owner-led test (finding 3) and compute the break-even (finding 1).
