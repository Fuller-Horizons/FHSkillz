# Which Long Island pool-service number to use

## Part 1: Answer

I can't tell you which one is right, and I'd advise against putting either on the slide as a plain fact. Neither number can be traced to a source today.

- **$410M (your partner):** This is **User-provided**. It came from a trade association deck that no one can find, so it can't be checked. I did find the likely association, the Long Island Pool & Spa Association, a NESPA/PHTA chapter covering Nassau and Suffolk [5]. I found no market-size figure from it.
- **$265M (you):** This is **User-provided**. It comes from a regional bank report you saw last year. You haven't named the bank or the report, and I found no such report online.
- **Wider search:** I found no published Long Island residential pool-service market figure at all. Search returned only national numbers.
  - IBISWorld puts U.S. pool cleaning services at about $7.2B for 2024 [1].
  - Arizton puts U.S. pool maintenance and cleaning at $8.08B for 2023, with residential the largest segment [2].
  - Those two disagree with each other, probably because they define the market differently. So "pool service" has no single definition even nationally.

The two figures differ by about $145M, so a few things could explain the gap:
- **Different scope:** one may count only cleaning and chemicals, and the other may add repairs, openings and closings, or equipment.
- **Different year:** one report may be older than the other.
- **Different geography:** one may cover a wider region than Long Island.
- **Bad provenance:** the trade deck may have been a vendor or promotional slide.

None of these can be settled until someone finds the originals.

**What I'd put on the slide:** Show both as an unverified range ("$265M–$410M") with a footnote that says "sources unverified." Or leave the figure out until it's resolved. Don't pick one on feel. If the number is load-bearing for the client's decision, build it bottom-up, as in the packet below.
