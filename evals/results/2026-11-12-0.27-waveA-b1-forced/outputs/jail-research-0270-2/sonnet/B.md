# Which Long Island pool-service number to use

**Neither number is verified, so I can't tell you which one is right. I wouldn't put either on the slide as it stands.** A sanity check suggests both are probably too high, and the $410M figure is the weaker of the two.

## Part 1: Synthesized answer

- **Your partner's $410M has no source.** The trade-association deck is gone, so there is no document, date or method to check. That makes it Unknown. A figure you can't trace can't be defended if the client asks where it came from.
- **Your $265M has a named source type but no document either.** A regional bank report is a better source type, but you saw it last year and don't have it in hand. I couldn't find it in this session, so it's also Unknown for now. It's stale if it's more than 12 months old.
- **I found no Long Island figure.** I searched for a Long Island pool-service market size and a bank industry report, and found nothing specific to Long Island. The results were national only [1][2].
- **Both figures look high against national data.** This is my own estimate, not a sourced fact.
  - National pool-service estimates run from about $7.2B (IBISWorld, 2024) [1] to about $8.1B (Arizton, 2023) [2]. A third, narrower figure is about $2.5B for direct residential service [3].
  - Long Island has roughly 2.9M people, about 0.9% of the US. That population is from my memory and I haven't sourced it. At that share, the national range implies roughly $60–70M, or about $22M on the narrow definition.
  - Pool density on Long Island is likely above average, but $265M would imply a national market around $30B, about four times the published estimates. $410M implies about $47B.
  - A definition mismatch could explain part of the gap. "Pool service" in one source may include repairs, openings and closings, chemicals or construction, and the other may not.
- **The two numbers may not measure the same thing.** Before choosing, you'd want to know what each one includes.

**My recommendation:** don't use either figure as a sourced fact on the slide. You can build a defensible number from the bottom up, or find the original sources.
