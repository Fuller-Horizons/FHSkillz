# Newsletter item (draft, not yet cleared to publish)

**Audience row used:** Line manager (half page, light translation), adapted for an all-staff readership that includes part-timers and contractors.

**What happened in October, and what we're doing about it**

A file share set up with the wrong sharing settings left a document containing customer information reachable by anyone with its link for about six days in October. The share was locked down on October 20. Our legal team has been notified and is deciding what, if anything, we need to tell the affected customers. We won't speculate about that outcome here.

The business stake is trust. Customers hand us their information on the understanding that we protect it, and a mistake like this can cost us customers and create legal liability. We're also telling you plainly because the cause was ordinary: a sharing setting that was wrong and went unnoticed.

IT has since turned on automatic expiry for share links across the company, so a link no longer works forever. IT will also audit all shared files every quarter.

**What we're asking of you**
- When you share a file, choose "specific people" rather than "anyone with the link" whenever you can.
- If you see a share that looks too open, or a file with customer information in the wrong place, report it to IT right away. You won't be in trouble for flagging it.
- If a customer or outside party asks you about this, don't answer. Send them to Legal.

**Decision this item serves:** whether to publish the incident to the whole company before Legal decides on customer notification.
**Recommendation (high confidence):** publish only after Legal approves the wording, and not before. Telling every employee, including contractors, ahead of any customer notice risks the story reaching customers or the public first. Skipping the item altogether means staff don't learn the new share habits.
**Deadline:** the next newsletter deadline. Delay costs little, since the fixes are already live.
