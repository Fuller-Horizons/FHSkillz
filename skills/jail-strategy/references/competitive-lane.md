# COMPETITIVE lane — detail

Pattern adapted from ECC (github.com/affaan-m/ECC, MIT, © 2026 Affaan Mustafa).
Rewritten for FHSkillz; the core rules stay inline in SKILL.md.

## Step A — Pin the frame first
Before listing any rival, capture from the user or client: what they sell,
to whom, the differentiator they believe in, and the decision this serves
(for example, "add commercial service or not"). If you do not have these,
ask. A rival list built without this frame is noise. Record the answers as
**User-provided**.

## Step B — Scope the competitor set
For each candidate, note size and model, niche overlap, geography, price and
engagement model, and how they win customers. Then place each in one tier:

| Tier | Test | Inclusion reason (required) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Direct | Same offer, same buyers, same area | one line |
| Adjacent | Overlaps on one capability or one buyer type | one line |
| Aspirational | Not a rival today; sets the bar for where you are heading | one line |

Prune to the rivals that teach the most. A short, defensible set beats a
census. List substitutes (in-house teams, DIY, new technology) as a threat
vector with a note, not as profiled rivals, unless they are material.
Where to look: company sites, case studies, review directories, licensing or
permit records, job postings, public filings (SEC EDGAR for public peers),
and customer interviews.

## Step C — Five Forces from evidence
| Force | Evidence to look for (examples) |
| :--- | :--- |
| New entrants | New business applications (Census Business Formation Statistics); licensing or capital barriers; time to first revenue |
| Supplier power | Input price trends (BLS Producer Price Index); number of suppliers; switching cost |
| Buyer power | Customer concentration in the client's own book (User-provided); contract length; price transparency |
| Substitutes | Customer interviews or survey answers; alternative products; in-house option |
| Rivalry | Establishment counts by industry and county (Census County Business Patterns, Economic Census); rival pricing and capacity; growth rate |

Rules:
1. Rate each force **High / Medium / Low** only from evidence.
2. **Two or more evidence items per force.** Fewer means the rating is
   `Unknown (searched: <what you tried>)`. State what would resolve it.
3. Each evidence item carries its label (Fact with `[n]`, User-provided,
   Estimate, Inference). Judgment-only support caps the rating at Low
   confidence.
4. One line on what the rating means for the decision. A force that does not
   touch the decision is noted and not expanded.
5. Do not add an overall "industry attractiveness score". Say which forces
   drive the decision.

## Step D — Profile cards
One card per profiled rival, same layout every time.

```
## <Rival>
- Tier: <Direct | Adjacent | Aspirational> — <inclusion reason>
- Model / size / area: <short>
- Positioning in their words: <one line>
- Named customers or proof: <item> — proven | asserted

| Dimension | Score 1-5 | Why (one line) | Source [n] | Proven / asserted |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: |

- Where they are strong: <Fact/Inference>
- Where they are exposed: <Fact/Inference>
- What we do not know: <Unknown + how to resolve>
```

Pick 5-8 dimensions that match the decision (for example: offer clarity,
proof and reviews, capacity, price signal, service area, speed to quote).
Score scale: 1 absent or generic · 2 below par · 3 competent, table stakes ·
4 strong, a buyer would cite it · 5 best in class. Anchor every score to
something observable. Self-reported claims with no corroboration are
**asserted** and scored lower on proof dimensions.

Bias controls:
- **No composite score.** Show the dimension scores. A weighted average hides
  the point.
- **Asserted vs proven.** Website copy is marketing, not fact.
- **Affinity bias.** Score craft and results separately. A dull site may be
  winning larger jobs.
- **Flashiness.** Awards and polish are not proof of commercial depth.
- **Survivorship.** The well-marketed rivals are not the whole market. Look
  at directories and permit or license lists for quiet operators.
- **Calibrate.** Re-read all scores side by side. A "4" must mean the same
  thing for every rival.

## Step E — Implications
1. Where the client is strong against the Direct tier, and where exposed.
2. Which one or two forces drive the decision, and why.
3. White space (an unserved buyer or need) or the exposed flank, each backed
   by cited entries.
4. The decision step: go, no-go, or go-with-conditions, plus the first
   actions and the signals that would change the call.
5. Watch-list: three or fewer observable rival moves (a hire, a price change,
   a new license) with how to check them.

## Numbers rule
No market size, share, or revenue figure without a citation or an
**Estimate** label (method + inputs). If neither exists, write the claim
without the number.
