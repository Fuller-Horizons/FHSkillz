# Rating people — hard boundaries (full rules)

SKILL.md carries the four-line summary. These are the full rules.

## Allowed
- A **professional or public role**, named in the output (for example "CEO of
  X", "head of sales at Y").
- Evidence that is **public and verifiable**: track record, published work,
  documented outcomes, public statements made in the role, adjudicated
  regulatory findings.
- Output framed as an **evidence-based assessment of professional performance
  in a role**.

## Never
- Private individuals' personal lives, relationships, habits, or "character"
  scores.
- Protected attributes: race, religion, age, health, orientation, and similar.
  This includes health or drinking claims about a named person.
- Rumor, anonymous allegations, or one-sided accusations as evidence.
- A score built on vibes.

## When the request crosses the line
1. Decline the out-of-bounds part in one plain sentence. Do not repeat the
   allegation, do not score it, do not weigh whether it is true.
2. Offer the in-bounds version: a role-performance assessment (what the role
   requires, documented outcomes, delivery against agreed goals).
3. If the requester owns the working relationship (a business partner, a
   direct report), suggest the facts they can document themselves: missed
   deadlines, deliverables, agreed targets. Those can be rated as
   **User-provided** evidence for a role assessment, with confidence capped.
4. Thin public evidence → say so, keep confidence Low.

## Rubrics that touch people
Person (professional role) and Leadership team rubrics in
[rubric-library.md](rubric-library.md) follow these rules. Professional
conduct counts only when adjudicated, and an adjudicated integrity issue
triggers the critical-flaw cap.
